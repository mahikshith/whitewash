# Whitewash product discovery and implementation proposal

18 October 2026

Whitewash can become a useful writing and document editing product. The strongest first version helps people improve stiff drafts while preserving their meaning, voice, and supported formatting. A universal promise to remove AI watermarks, pass every detector, and preserve every document exactly is not a credible product specification.

Start with a mobile-friendly web editor for text and Markdown. Validate editing quality and willingness to pay, then add a carefully bounded Word workflow. Build a store-distributed mobile app after repeat use establishes which mobile features matter.

## Technical feasibility

Four different problems sit inside the original idea.

| Capability | Feasibility | Honest product promise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inspect unusual Unicode and clean copy-paste artifacts | High, with language-aware rules | Show exactly which characters change and allow undo |
| Improve clarity, rhythm, and personal voice | High, with review and evaluation | Suggest natural edits and protect specified content |
| Estimate whether prose resembles AI output | Possible, with substantial uncertainty | Provide a fallible signal with scope and an inconclusive result |
| Verify a particular provider watermark | Depends on authorized detector access | Report that provider's verification result and its limits |
| Remove selected file metadata | High for explicitly supported fields and formats | Inspect, remove selected fields, and verify the exported copy |
| Guarantee universal detector bypass | Not supportable | Do not sell this guarantee |
| Preserve arbitrary documents perfectly while rewriting | Not supportable across all formats | Publish supported structures and flag exceptions |

### Watermarks and invisible characters

Anthropic's August 2026 explanation says its text watermark uses patterns in word selection and adds no hidden characters. Verification requires the corresponding key; the announced detection API is in private preview for eligible organizations. Removing unusual spaces therefore does not remove this watermark. Watermark evidence also cannot prove that a document was wholly generated by Claude. [Anthropic explanation](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark)

Google's SynthID research demonstrates another word-selection watermark. The public reference implementation needs a matching configuration, and its Bayesian detector is trained for a particular key. Installing it does not provide the production keys for arbitrary providers. [SynthID Text repository](https://github.com/google-deepmind/synthid-text)

An editing model can itself generate watermarked output. We must evaluate the chosen provider's behavior before making any claim about provenance after rewriting. Natural wording and a low classifier score do not establish human authorship.

Character cleanup still solves a useful, separate problem. Show zero-width characters, nonbreaking spaces, soft hyphens, inconsistent punctuation, and line-break artifacts in context. Some are legitimate: joiners affect scripts and emoji, nonbreaking spaces affect typography, and indentation can carry meaning. Do not remove every non-ASCII character or apply compatibility normalization indiscriminately. Offer punctuation preferences without presenting ordinary punctuation as proof of AI use.

### Detection limitations

OpenAI withdrew its 2023 classifier because of low accuracy. This is historical evidence of the difficulty, not a measurement of every 2026 detector. Research by Liang and colleagues found false positives affecting non-native English writers among the detectors they tested. Those findings justify evaluation on our intended users rather than assuming a vendor accuracy claim transfers to them. [OpenAI classifier notice](https://openai.com/index/new-ai-classifier-for-indicating-ai-written-text/), [Liang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02819)

If detection is included, show the provider and version, supported language, sample requirements, and whether the result is a classifier estimate or watermark verification. A model confidence score, the percentage of sentences flagged, and a calibrated probability of authorship are different quantities. Never relabel one as another. Unsupported languages, short inputs, and ambiguous results should produce an inconclusive result. A negative watermark check means no evidence from that check, not proof of human origin.

Keep optional detector calls separate from rewriting. Sending every document to several services increases disclosure, latency, and cost; averaging their scores does not create a reliable probability. Avoid an automatic rewrite-and-rescan loop optimized solely to reduce a detector score.

## Product directions

| Direction | User value | Commercial outlook | Main concern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free text cleanup utility | Repair copied text and inspect unusual characters | Useful acquisition feature | Easy to reproduce and hard to charge much for alone |
| Personal writing editor | Make recurring emails, reports, and proposals sound like the writer | Best initial paid-product hypothesis | Must outperform a generic editing prompt |
| Document editing with preservation | Reduce editing and formatting repair time | Potentially stronger professional value | More engineering and support per format |
| Detector and provenance service | Help people interpret origin signals | Separate workflow, potentially useful later | Reliability, access, and customer expectations |

Recommended initial audience: freelancers and small agencies producing recurring English client reports and proposals, including people writing in English as an additional language. They have repeat work and a concrete cost when a tool changes facts or breaks formatting. This is a customer hypothesis to test, not established demand.

Students can benefit from clarity editing, but a business centered on passing institutional detectors depends on unpredictable scores and promises we cannot control. Preserve citations and quotations, support disclosure where required, and distinguish editing assistance from proof of original authorship.

## Proposed first experience

The first screen should contain an editor, an optional audience/style choice, and one primary action. Provide two clearly separated operations: Clean text for deterministic character and spacing fixes, and Improve writing for model-assisted edits. Introduce a detector panel only if users demonstrate a separate need for it.

Desktop can show original and edited text side by side. Mobile should use accessible Original, Edited, and Changes tabs. Provide a sample document so visitors can understand the product without sharing their own work.

The useful features, in priority order, are:

1. Visible changes with accept, reject, undo, and an always-available original.
2. User-selected locks for names, numbers, units, citations, quotations, URLs, and technical terms. Warn or reject an edit that violates a lock.
3. A small set of editing choices: light polish, plain language, and a saved personal voice based on samples supplied by the user.
4. A character inspector that explains each cleanup instead of silently deleting text.
5. Text and Markdown import/export with explicit protection for code and link destinations.
6. A supported Word workflow, once preservation checks pass.
7. Metadata & Privacy: inspect supported file metadata, remove selected fields, and show a before/after export report, with the boundaries below.

For example, a draft might say, "We are delighted to inform you that the revised estimate is INR 48,500, valid until 21 October." A useful edit is "The revised estimate is INR 48,500 and is valid until 21 October." The interface should show the removed filler and confirm the protected amount and date stayed unchanged. This demonstrates observable value without a detector score.

Later candidates include client glossaries, selected-section rewriting, document batches, team voice guides, browser integration, and a mobile share sheet. Version history should be opt-in with clear deletion controls. An edit record can document actions inside our application; it cannot certify the origin of text pasted into it.

Avoid deliberately introducing mistakes, invented personal anecdotes, fabricated citations, or mechanical synonym replacement. Those changes can make writing less accurate and less useful.

## Format support and preservation

Preserving a file extension, preserving document structure, and preserving page layout are separate promises. Longer replacement text can change pagination even when all styles survive.

| Format | Proposed support | Required boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pasted text and TXT | First release | Preserve paragraphs and meaningful whitespace; disclose intentional cleanup |
| Markdown | First release after parser checks | Edit prose nodes; protect code, URLs, tables, and frontmatter by default |
| DOCX | Next release with a published support matrix | Preserve supported paragraphs, runs, lists, tables, headers, footers, links, and fields; reject unsupported constructs |
| Text PDF | Later import and reviewed export | Reliable extraction does not guarantee reconstruction of the original page |
| Scanned PDF | Separate later feature | OCR adds recognition errors and requires preview and correction |
| PDF forms and signed PDFs | Defer specialized handling | Preserve field behavior where supported; editing can invalidate signatures |
| PPTX | Later, selected text boxes | Check overflow and render slides; retain masters and objects |
| XLSX | Later, selected prose cells | Protect formulas, data types, numeric values, named ranges, and workbook features |

The python-docx API explicitly documents that assigning paragraph text collapses existing runs and removes character formatting. A naive paragraph replacement would violate our promise. Use a structured editing plan and patch supported document parts, with tests for text spanning several runs. [python-docx API](https://python-docx.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/text.html)

PDFs store positioned content and do not reliably expose semantic paragraphs. Scans need OCR. Extraction libraries cannot restore the author's original document structure automatically. [pypdf explanation](https://pypdf.readthedocs.io/en/stable/user/extract-text.html)

The openpyxl documentation warns that unsupported workbook objects, including shapes, can be lost on save. For rich spreadsheets, narrowly patching supported OOXML parts or using a fidelity-oriented document SDK may be preferable; compare options on representative files before choosing. [openpyxl tutorial](https://openpyxl.readthedocs.io/en/stable/tutorial.html)

Keep the uploaded original immutable. Display unsupported features before processing, write a new output, and reopen and validate it before offering download. Where faithful editing is unavailable, offer an explicit text-only conversion rather than silently degrading the file.

## Metadata and Privacy feature

Add this feature to the planned product scope. It creates a separate copy with selected metadata removed and tells the user what the exporter checked. Its value is controlled disclosure when sharing documents. It must distinguish removable file properties, embedded content credentials, visible document watermarks, and statistical text watermarks.

### Removal scope

| Information | Planned behavior | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Author, last editor, creator application, dates, and custom properties | Inspect and allow selective removal in supported formats | These fields are not inherently AI evidence; file-system or service dates are separate |
| PDF document properties and XMP metadata | Inspect both stores and remove selected supported values | Clearing only one store is insufficient; do not advertise forensic erasure |
| Office comments, tracked changes, notes, hidden sheets, and embedded objects | Show separately in the inspection report | These can contain substantive content; removal needs a distinct user selection and preview |
| Embedded C2PA or other supported provenance metadata | Identify it; offer explicit removal only for formats we can validate | Removal discards provenance information; external copies or bindings may remain |
| Unusual Unicode characters in text | Use the existing reviewed cleanup operation | Preserve meaningful Unicode; this does not remove a word-selection watermark |
| Statistical LLM text watermark | Mark removal as unverified and outside the metadata-cleaning guarantee | Do not label the output watermark-free based on metadata absence or an ordinary detector score |
| Visible logos, attribution, or page watermarks | Leave intact in this feature | They are content, not ordinary file metadata |

The PDF implementation must handle both regular document metadata and XMP. The pypdf documentation describes these as separate stores. [PDF metadata documentation](https://pypdf.readthedocs.io/en/stable/user/metadata.html)

Office inspection also needs format-specific coverage: Microsoft lists properties, comments, revisions, hidden content, and embedded objects among inspection categories. Removing them all indiscriminately could alter the user's document. [Microsoft Document Inspector](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/office/vba/library-reference/concepts/using-the-document-inspector)

C2PA supports recovery of a manifest separated from an asset through soft bindings and a manifest repository. Removing an embedded credential therefore cannot establish that every provenance association has disappeared. Do not query external recovery services without user authorization to transmit the necessary data. [C2PA manifest recovery specification](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/softbinding/Decoupled.html)

### User experience and export checks

Use a Metadata & Privacy panel with Inspect, Remove selected metadata, and Download cleaned copy actions. Show field categories and values before processing. After export, label each category Removed and verified, Retained, Unsupported, or Not checked. A result such as "Selected author and creator fields removed; text watermark status not checked" is meaningful. "All AI traces permanently erased" is unsupported.

Keep this operation independent of rewriting and avoid model calls. Process locally where practical; disclose when a server receives the file. Preserve the original, and do not create a persistent metadata report containing removed personal information unless the user chooses to save one. Do not substitute invented author names, dates, human-authorship declarations, or forged credentials for removed fields.

Start with inspection and selected properties in the supported DOCX workflow. Add simple unsigned PDFs only after round-trip preservation checks. Follow with PPTX and XLSX as their adapters mature. Plain pasted text has no original document metadata to erase, and Markdown frontmatter is content requiring an explicit selection.

Reject or clearly report unsupported encrypted, signed, protected, or structurally complex documents. Signed documents need specialized handling because editing may invalidate signatures. Changes affecting archival/accessibility conformance need their own validation. Do not silently flatten a file or lose formulas, form fields, links, notes, or accessibility structure to claim a successful cleanup.

Before release, test files with distinctive values planted in every supported metadata store. Reopen the output with an independent inspection path, check selected values are absent and retained values remain, and verify supported content and rendering. Check nested objects and historical PDF data before expanding any erasure claim; merely making an entry inaccessible through the usual API is not sufficient evidence. The writer must not silently reintroduce selected metadata. Any removal failure must prevent a success label.

Scope the result to the exported copy and inspected stores. It does not erase cloud history, backups, provider records, operating-system attributes, external credentials, or information in the document body. This is an added product specification, not an implemented sanitizer or a validated watermark-removal engine.

A closed-source product's public interface, help pages, and documented API can inform our design. Its private model, training data, and source code remain inaccessible without authorization. Do not infer its architecture from marketing or copy proprietary assets. Code, model-weight, dataset, and service licenses require separate checks before commercial reuse.

## Proposed implementation

Use a TypeScript web application with React and a small server API. Next.js is a reasonable candidate already covered by installed skills, but stack selection should follow the first prototype's needs. Begin with one application and one model provider selected by evaluation. Do not add an agent framework, vector database, custom model training, or a multi-provider routing layer at this stage.

The initial processing sequence is:

1. Validate input size and encoding, and identify the selected editing operation.
2. Parse content into stable blocks and retain an immutable original.
3. Identify protected spans and apply only approved deterministic cleanup rules.
4. Send editable prose and bounded context to the model, requesting edits associated with block IDs.
5. Validate the response schema, block mapping, protected values, and text completeness. Fail visibly or retain the original when checks fail.
6. Show a diff for user review and export accepted changes.

Preservation checks catch identifiable changes, not every shift in meaning. Keep human review in the workflow and evaluate semantic errors explicitly. A second model's agreement is not proof that an edit is correct.

Add a Python worker when Word/PDF processing justifies it. Use format-specific adapters that retain source structure; do not flatten every upload to text and attempt to recreate its formatting from scratch. Long document jobs can then use a queue, private object storage, expiry, cancellation, and bounded retries. Short text requests do not need that infrastructure initially.

Keep API credentials on the server. Treat document content as untrusted input, including embedded instructions. Uploaded files need type/signature checks, size and decompression limits, isolated parsing, and no macro execution or unsolicited network access. Escape document previews and avoid logging their bodies. An anonymous free feature needs rate limits and a bounded spend budget; billing later needs verified webhooks and idempotent credit changes.

A responsive web product reaches desktop and phone users first. A later Expo/React Native app could share the API, validation rules, and account entitlements, while adding share-sheet import and native document handling. A PWA is an interim installable web experience, not an App Store app. Choose a native stack only when the mobile use case is clear.

## Privacy and launch implications

Privacy is a product feature only when the full processing chain supports the claim. Local Unicode cleanup can stay in the browser. Hosted rewriting discloses the selected text to a provider; explain that boundary, negotiate appropriate retention terms, and obtain consent where required. Deleting our stored file does not establish deletion from every downstream processor. Avoid persistent document history by default and provide an explicit save option.

Apple's review rules require disclosure and explicit permission before personal data is shared with third-party AI. Design that consent before the first relevant transmission in a native app. [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

The European Commission explains that Article 50 contains marking obligations for providers of generative AI systems and an exception for standard assistive editing. Our obligations depend on the actual service and markets, not the word "editor" in its branding. Obtain a launch-specific legal assessment before offering broad rewriting or watermark-related claims in the EU. [Commission guidance](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act)

Before a paid launch, resolve upload rights, subprocessors, deletion periods, account security, refunds, local tax/payment support, and mobile-store billing requirements for the actual markets. Treat Whitewash as a working name; its association with concealment may conflict with a trustworthy editing product. Naming and trademark clearance remain separate decisions.

## Monetization and retention hypotheses

Keep deterministic cleanup free. Offer a limited amount of writing improvement that lets a person finish a real task before encountering an upgrade. Paid value can include larger supported documents, saved voice profiles, reusable client glossaries, and batch workflows.

Use one entitlement system across web and mobile if both are released. Start with transparent usage credits or a monthly allowance. Price professional document work separately if its processing and support costs differ substantially. Avoid unlimited plans until measured usage supports them, and avoid lifetime access to an uncapped recurring model cost.

The cost model is:

Processing cost = input tokens x input rate + output tokens x output rate + validation/retries + optional detection + extraction/OCR + storage and delivery.

Contribution = net receipts after tax and payment/store fees - variable processing costs - refunds - variable support costs.

Illustration only: if net receipts are $10 per month and variable costs are $2, contribution is $8 before fixed operating costs and acquisition. If costs rise to $7, only $3 remains. These are assumptions, not provider quotes or a proposed price. Measure actual accepted-output cost before setting plans.

Retention should come from less work on the next real document: remembered preferences, client terminology, reusable voices, and convenient import/export. Ask for feedback after a completed task. Measure weekly repeat use among people with recurring writing needs; daily streaks are poorly matched to occasional reports.

The differentiator should be evidence that users spend less time repairing wording, facts, and formatting than with their current workflow. Voice matching alone is already offered by large competitors. A generic model prompt in an attractive box is easy to replace.

## Validation and proposed delivery sequence

The following are planning estimates for one experienced developer with access to model APIs and representative files, not delivery commitments. Broad document fidelity can extend the schedule considerably.

| Phase | Indicative timing | Evidence needed before expanding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer and quality investigation | Week 1 | Interview 10 to 15 target users; obtain consented examples; compare existing tools and a generic editing prompt |
| Text and Markdown prototype | Weeks 2 to 3 | Reviewable edits, protected spans, reliable export, input limits, deletion behavior |
| Bounded Word pilot and payment trial | Weeks 4 to 6 if Word tests pass | Supported-file matrix, representative render checks, measured costs, real payment commitments |
| Native app, more formats, or detection | Later, based on pilot evidence | Repeat demand that justifies each additional maintenance burden |

Build an initial evaluation set of at least 100 varied text passages and a separate set of representative documents. Include human-written text, AI drafts, mixed editing, non-native English, short messages, technical prose, quotations, negation, numbers, and Unicode-heavy examples. Keep authors and document templates separated across tuning and evaluation. Expand the corpus before making broad performance claims.

Proposed internal quality gates are zero protected-span changes in the test set, no silent corruption of supported documents, and a clear blind preference over a generic editing prompt. A possible initial preference target is 70%, but report sample size and uncertainty; do not market a small pilot result as universal performance. Test links, fields, formulas, list numbering, and pagination as applicable, with rendered previews for document formats.

For detection, report false positives and true positives at the chosen threshold, broken down by language, length, and source type. Keep out-of-domain and inconclusive cases visible. Include current model outputs alongside public benchmarks. Do not use only the detector being displayed in our own UI as the evaluation judge.

For demand, recruit roughly 20 qualified pilot users and seek at least five real payment commitments, plus repeat use on a second genuine task. These are decision thresholds for an early experiment, not evidence of product-market fit. Track time to first accepted result, accepted/rejected edits, corrections after export, return use, and processing cost. Collect event metadata without capturing document bodies by default.

Stop or change direction if users prefer a generic prompt, if preservation errors remain common, or if payment interest depends entirely on a universal bypass guarantee. Proceed when users repeatedly pay for better writing and less document repair.
